A fair six-sided die has six equally likely outcomes, numbered one through six. The probability of any one face is one sixth. When two fair dice are rolled independently, there are thirty-six equally likely ordered pairs. Six pairs have a sum of seven: one and six, two and five, three and four, four and three, five and two, and six and one. Consequently, the probability of a sum of seven is one sixth. The probability of at least one six can be found by subtracting the probability of no six from one. Both dice avoid six with probability twenty-five thirty-sixths, so at least one six occurs with probability eleven thirty-sixths. Independence means the result of the first die does not change the probabilities of the second die.
