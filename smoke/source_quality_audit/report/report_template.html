One audit, two useful views

The 100-per-source design gives small sources a meaningful inspection budget. It also overrepresents them relative to training. The raw sample rate is 26.1%; weighting each source by its actual population gives a 27.2% major-flag estimate.

Minor flags describe nonmaterial weaknesses. Uncertain records remain unresolved. Neither category should be silently merged into “correct” or “incorrect.” The overall label gives priority to major defects, then uncertainty, then minor defects.

Prioritise by exposure

The largest rate is not the largest problem

WildChat and OpenCodeInstruct together represent 39.3% of the retained data and contribute 14.22 percentage points to the 27.2% major-flag estimate. They account for @@TOP_TWO@@% of estimated major flags, making them the first places to investigate.

High impact

WildChat

31/100 major flags across a source that supplies 24.9% of the retained data. Answer correctness has 23 major flags; prompt quality has four. A prompt-only repair will not address the whole issue.

High impact

OpenCodeInstruct

45/100 major flags and 14.5% of the dataset. Prompt quality has 32 major flags, answer correctness 29. Examples show contradictory specifications and responses fitted to bad sample outputs.

High rate, tiny share

Nemotron SWE v1

100/100 major flags, but only 141 retained records—0.0024% of the dataset. Tool actions and incomplete agent workflows are a poor fit for this standalone-answer audit. Separate that format before treating the source as universally defective.

Contribution is a prioritisation estimate, not an attribution of cause. The same original prompt can acquire defects through extraction, response generation, import, or parsing.

03
What the reviewer sees

Answer problems dominate; input problems also matter

Answer correctness has 613 major flags, covering 69.0% of all major-flagged records. Prompt quality has 331. The sets overlap: 149 records have both, while 464 have an answer major flag without a prompt major flag.

Conversely, 182 records have a prompt major flag without an answer major flag. Some candidates correctly identify an ill-posed question. In total, 275 of the 888 major-flagged records have no major answer-correctness flag: whole-record suitability and final-answer correctness are different measurements.

Structural patterns measured directly in the sample

535

Fenced prompts without line breaks

All 3,400 sampled prompts are single-line strings; 535 contain triple-backtick fences. This warrants checking extraction against originals, especially for indentation-sensitive code. It does not establish that every one of these prompts is unusable.

35

Reasoning markers inside final answers

35 answers contain the literal <|channel>thought marker. All belong to Nemotron SWE v1. Inspect their multi-channel boundaries rather than blindly deleting tokens.

81

Answers containing a tool-call marker

81 contain <|tool_call>; 70 receive overall major flags. The remaining 11 show why a marker alone is not a reliable quality-rejection rule. Preserve task-format distinctions.

Longer traces deserve attention, not automatic removal

Major flags occur on 50.7% of sampled records with reasoning of at least 10,000 characters, versus 14.2% below 2,000. The intermediate bins are not monotonic.

This is a descriptive association in the equal-source sample. Source, task difficulty, and format can explain part of it. The audit does not establish that long reasoning causes bad answers or justify a blanket length cutoff.

07
A practical response

Repair mechanisms before removing whole sources

1

Separate agent trajectories from standalone answers

Inspect the SWE records and route tool-dependent examples into a representation that retains the required environment, tools, and subsequent turns. Quarantine incomplete standalone exports; do not describe the entire original source as wrong. This is a narrow, low-volume issue with a strong sample signal.

2

Recover prompt structure from originals

Prioritise OpenCodeInstruct and OpenMathInstruct-2. Compare original questions, choices, equations, images, code indentation, and sample outputs with our extracted prompts. Restore context where possible; avoid guessing missing content or regenerating responses to a broken specification.

3

Target answer checks where the population impact is largest

Focus the first deeper validation on WildChat and OpenCodeInstruct. Use task-specific tests, numerical checks, or a human-reviewed subset to validate the model’s flags before deciding on rejection thresholds. Do not equate model agreement, nonempty outputs, or successful code execution with correctness.

4

Audit boundary handling and repetitive traces

Investigate the 35 final-answer channel-marker cases and concrete repetitive traces. Compare raw and parsed responses before changing parser behavior. A long trace or tool marker on its own is not sufficient evidence for deletion.

5

Measure the effect on a fresh sample

After a repair, resample retained outputs per source and compare quality flags, useful-data loss, source mixture, and cost. Keep uncertainty separate and use untouched controls. Downstream student training is needed for a learning-value claim.

These are proposed follow-ups. This analysis changed neither pipeline code nor dataset records and ran no new model reviews, code-execution tests, or downstream training.

08
How to read this audit

Reproducible sampling, bounded conclusions

Population and sampling

The input is stage6_v2_decontam/clean: 196 JSONL shards, 5,831,367 records, and 34 source labels. A streaming reservoir selects 100 physical records per source without replacement, using seed 20261026 and independent source RNGs. The sample, row locations, raw-line hashes, and file hashes are frozen.

Different splits with the same stored source name are pooled. Duplicate prompt IDs are not silently removed from the population. These are prompt-origin labels, not guaranteed original row/revision identifiers or teacher identities.

Review protocol

GPT-6.1 Sol, high reasoning effort, received full prompts, reasoning, final answers, and raw responses, with source names hidden. It assessed six dimensions without tools, external lookups, or execution. One accepted model assessment is retained per record; no second reviewer or human adjudication was performed.

All 629 batches are complete. Technical failures triggered retries. 56 batches covering 336 records needed recovery: valid whole reviews were preserved, and missing/invalid evidence was repaired using the same model. This is not an ensemble vote. Exact-quote validation proves that text is present, not that the reviewer’s interpretation is correct.

Weights and intervals

Let Nₛ be the population in source s and p̂ₛ its observed label rate. The dataset estimate is Σ (Nₛ / N) p̂ₛ. Its variance estimate is Σ (Nₛ/N)² (1 − nₛ/Nₛ) p̂ₛ(1−p̂ₛ)/(nₛ−1); displayed intervals use ±1.96 standard errors.

This treats source strata as sampled independently. Intervals capture sampling uncertainty conditional on fixed model labels; they do not include systematic reviewer error, uncertainty in causal attribution, or the effects of comparing many sources. Per-source table intervals use the simpler Wilson approximation.

What is still unknown

All sampled records lack a teacher-model field and finish reason. They also lack the newer per-record judge and grounding audit metadata. Existing fields cannot establish why a row passed filtering or whether a missing input originated upstream. The v2 filter report says zero records despite populated shards; the full scan does reconcile with the decontamination survivor count.

This audit covers retained data only. It cannot estimate useful examples wrongly rejected by filtering, certify a true correctness rate, establish the original sources’ quality, or demonstrate student-training gains. It evaluates the existing dataset, not a fresh run of the latest code.
