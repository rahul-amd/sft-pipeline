A stronger reviewer signal, with important gaps

SFT: calibrated Clef reaches MCC 0.398 versus 0.324 for calibrated Flash. Calibration raises Clef’s major-defect recall from 24.6% to 63.5%. Among its 791 no-defect decisions, 5.8% are Sol-major; only 70.3% match Sol’s no-defect label.

DOLCI: calibrated Clef reaches MCC 0.422 versus 0.297 for calibrated Flash. Calibration raises Clef’s major-defect recall from 36.0% to 79.2%. Among its 401 no-defect decisions, 8.0% are Sol-major; only 55.4% match Sol’s no-defect label.

The larger model improves agreement both before and after applying the same calibration procedure to Flash. Both paired 95% intervals for the calibrated MCC gain exclude zero. However, calibration reduces minor-defect recall and misses every Sol-uncertain case in the held-out set. It is useful evidence for a review-routing experiment, not validation of an automatic acceptance filter.

Limits and next decision

All results describe agreement with one frozen model reviewer. Human adjudication of a blinded disagreement sample is still needed to distinguish reviewer errors from classifier errors. The calibrator targets the balanced audit mixture; probabilities may need adjustment when deployed on a different source mixture. No new threshold should be chosen from this held-out set and then reported as independently validated. The backbone’s position limit and successful full-input processing do not establish decision-head accuracy at long lengths; lifting the encoder’s 16k default is an explicit experimental intervention.

Use these results to decide whether a new calibration-and-routing study is justified. Choose its missed-defect target and review budget in advance, then evaluate the selected policy on fresh records. No pipeline filter or production behavior was changed in this experiment.
